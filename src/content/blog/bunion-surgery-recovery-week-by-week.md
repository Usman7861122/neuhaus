**Short answer:** bunion surgery recovery is usually a gradual process. Many people walk in a protective shoe or boot soon after surgery, wear regular shoes again after about two to three months, and feel fully healed in about six months to a year. Swelling often takes the longest to go away.

Your surgeon's plan comes first. The timeline below is a general guide, because the type of surgery and your own healing change what happens when.

## Before you begin

Surgery is usually a same-day procedure with no hospital stay. If you are still deciding whether you need it, read [3 signs it's time to consider bunion surgery](/blog/3-signs-its-time-to-consider-bunion-surgery) and our page on [bunions](/conditions/bunions/).

Get ready at home before surgery day:

- Arrange a ride home and some help for the first few days
- Set up a spot to rest with pillows so you can raise the foot
- Move things you use often to waist height
- Have easy meals, ice packs and any prescribed medicine ready

## Week 1: rest and raise the foot

- Your foot is in a bandage, splint or surgical shoe.
- Keep the foot raised above heart level as much as you can. This lowers swelling and pain.
- Take pain medicine as directed.
- Walk only as your surgeon says. Some people use crutches or a knee scooter.
- Keep the bandage clean and dry.

Call your surgeon if pain is out of control, the toe turns pale or blue, or you have a fever.

## Week 2: first check

- Many surgeons remove stitches around week two.
- You may start to move the toe gently if you are told to.
- Swelling and bruising are still normal. Bruises can spread to the other toes and ankle.

## Weeks 3 to 6: protected walking

- You usually keep wearing a surgical shoe or boot.
- You can often walk more, a little at a time, as your surgeon allows.
- Keep raising the foot at the end of the day.
- You may start simple toe exercises or physical therapy.

## Weeks 6 to 8: checking the bone

- An X-ray often shows how well the bone is healing.
- If healing looks good, you may start moving into a wide, soft shoe.
- Walk a bit further each week.

## Weeks 8 to 12: back to shoes

- Many people return to roomy sneakers or comfortable shoes.
- Swelling is still common at the end of the day.
- Low-impact exercise, such as cycling or swimming, is often allowed. Ask before running or jumping.

## Month 3 to 6 and beyond

- Swelling slowly settles. The foot can look and feel different from the other one for a while.
- Most people go back to their normal activities.
- Full healing and the final shape of the foot can take six months to a year.

## Tips for a smoother recovery

- Follow weight-bearing rules exactly. Walking on the foot too soon can harm bone healing.
- Wear wide shoes with room for your toes after surgery. See our [10 step guide for bunion pain](/blog/10-step-guide-to-treat-bunion-pain).
- Do not smoke, because it slows healing.
- Keep all follow-up visits, even if you feel good.
- If you have diabetes, keep your blood sugar well controlled.

## When to call your surgeon

- Fever or chills
- Redness spreading, pus or a bad smell
- Pain that gets worse instead of better
- Numb, cold, pale or blue toes
- Calf pain or swelling in the leg (go to the emergency room if you also have chest pain or trouble breathing)
