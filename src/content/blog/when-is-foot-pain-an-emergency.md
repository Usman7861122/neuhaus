**Short answer:** most foot pain is not an emergency. But a few signs mean you should go to the emergency room or call 911 right away. If you are not sure, it is safer to get checked.

This guide helps you sort foot problems into three groups: go to the ER now, see a podiatrist the same day, and book a regular visit.

## Go to the emergency room or call 911 if:

- A **bone is showing** through the skin, or the wound is deep or will not stop bleeding
- The foot or ankle looks **bent or out of shape** after an injury
- You **cannot put any weight** on the foot after a fall, a crash or a hard injury
- The foot is **cold, pale, blue or numb**, especially if it came on suddenly
- You have **redness spreading fast** with fever, chills or feeling very unwell
- Black or very dark skin appears on the foot or toes
- You have chest pain or trouble breathing along with leg or foot swelling

## See a podiatrist the same day if:

- You have **diabetes** and a new sore, blister, cut, or a foot that is hot, red and swollen
- A wound has **pus**, a bad smell, or is not healing
- You stepped on a **nail or sharp object** and the wound is deep (especially if you have diabetes)
- You have a very painful, swollen toe with a nail that is badly infected or ingrown. See [ingrown toenail removal](/blog/ingrown-toenail-removal-what-happens-and-recovery).
- You think you may have a **broken bone** but you can still walk, with swelling and bruising
- You have sudden, severe pain in the big toe with redness and warmth. This can be gout.

## Why diabetes changes the rules

If you have diabetes, nerve damage can hide pain, and poor blood flow slows healing. A small blister can turn into a serious infection. Do not wait to see if it gets better. ## Book a regular visit if:

- Pain lasts more than two weeks
- Heel pain is worst with the first steps in the morning ([plantar fasciitis](/blog/how-long-does-plantar-fasciitis-take-to-heal))
- A bunion, corn or callus hurts in shoes
- Your nails are thick, yellow or crumbly ([toenail fungus](/blog/toenail-fungus-treatments-that-actually-work))
- You keep rolling your ankle

## What to do while you wait for care

- Rest the foot and keep it raised
- For an injury, ice wrapped in a cloth for about 20 minutes at a time
- Cover any open wound with a clean dry bandage
- Do not pop blisters, cut into nails or try to treat a deep wound at home
- Do not drive yourself if you are in severe pain

If you cannot tell whether your foot pain is serious, call us or go to the emergency room. It is always better to be checked and told it is fine.
