**Short answer:** often no. There is no single rule for all of Tennessee, because a referral is usually a rule of your **insurance plan**, not of the podiatrist. Some plans let you book directly. Others want your primary care provider to send a referral first.

Below is a simple way to find out before you book, and what to expect with Medicare, TennCare and private insurance.

## Why do some plans ask for a referral?

Some health plans, such as HMO and some managed care plans, use your primary care provider (PCP) as the "gatekeeper." They want that doctor to approve a specialist visit first. Other plans, such as most PPOs, let you see a specialist without one.

A referral is not the same as **prior authorization**. A referral is a note from your PCP. Prior authorization is an approval from the insurance company, often for tests, surgery or special devices.

## What about Medicare?

- **Original Medicare:** you do not need a referral to see a podiatrist. Part B can cover medically needed foot care, such as treating an injury or a foot condition.
- **Routine foot care:** things like simple nail trimming and callus care are usually not covered, unless you have certain health problems such as diabetes with nerve damage or poor circulation.
- **Medicare Advantage:** these plans are run by private companies, and some do ask for referrals. Check your plan.

You can read Medicare's own page on [foot care coverage](https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/foot-care).

## What about TennCare?

TennCare members are covered through managed care health plans. Each plan has its own rules about referrals, approvals and which services are covered. Some plans ask you to see your PCP before a specialist, so do not guess.

## What about private insurance and employer plans?

- **PPO plans:** you can usually book a specialist yourself.
- **HMO and POS plans:** a referral from your PCP is often needed.
- **Any plan:** the podiatrist must be in your network, or you may pay more.

## 4 steps to check before you book

1. Look at the **member services phone number** on the back of your insurance card.
2. Ask: "Do I need a referral to see a podiatrist?"
3. Ask: "Is Neuhaus Foot & Ankle in my network?"
4. Ask: "Do I need approval for X-rays, orthotics or surgery?" Write down the date, the name of the person you spoke to, and what they said.

## When you should not wait for a referral

Some problems need quick care. If you have a wound that is not healing, signs of infection, or a sudden foot injury, call us or get urgent care first and sort out the paperwork after. See [when foot pain is an emergency](/blog/when-is-foot-pain-an-emergency).
