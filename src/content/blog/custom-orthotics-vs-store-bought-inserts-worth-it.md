**Short answer:** it depends on your foot problem. For common problems like mild heel pain, a good store-bought insert often works as well as a custom one. Custom orthotics are worth it when your foot has a specific problem, an unusual shape, or when cheaper inserts did not help.

We would rather tell you the honest answer than sell you something you do not need. Here is how to decide.

## What is the difference?

- **Store-bought inserts** are made in standard sizes and shapes. They give cushion or general arch support. They are cheap and easy to find.
- **Custom orthotics** are made from a mold, scan or exam of your own feet and are made to fit your foot and your problem. A podiatrist prescribes them after a full exam of how you stand and walk.

Read more in [how custom orthotics are different from store-bought](/blog/how-are-custom-orthotics-different-from-store-bought) and [how orthotics work](/blog/how-do-orthotics-work).

## What does the research say?

Studies that compared custom and ready-made inserts for common foot pain, such as heel pain, have often found similar results. That is good news if you have a simple problem: a well-chosen store-bought insert may be enough. It does not mean orthotics do not work. It means the right fit and the right problem matter more than the price tag.

## When store-bought inserts are a good place to start

- Mild or new heel or arch pain
- Tired feet from long days of standing
- You want extra cushion in a work or running shoe

Choose an insert that feels comfortable, matches your shoe size, and gives firm arch support. Give it two to four weeks. If you are not feeling better, see a podiatrist.

## When custom orthotics are often worth it

- You have **severe flat feet or very high arches**
- You have **posterior tibial tendon problems** or other tendon pain, or pain linked to the way your foot rolls in or out
- You have **diabetes** or poor sensation and need protection for your feet
- You have a foot that is a different shape or size than usual, or one leg that is longer
- Pain has lasted for months and **store-bought inserts have not helped**
- You have a condition like [bunions](/conditions/bunions/), [hammertoes](/conditions/hammertoes-mallet-toes-claw-toes-curly-toes/) or [metatarsalgia](/conditions/ball-of-foot-pain-metatarsalgia-forefoot-overload/) where the pressure points need to be shifted

## How much do they cost?

Store-bought inserts are usually quite cheap compared with custom ones. Custom orthotics cost more because of the exam, the molding and the lab work. Insurance coverage varies by plan, so ask before you order. We can check your benefits before your visit.

## How to get the most from your orthotics

- Wear them in shoes that fit and have room for them
- Break them in slowly over one to two weeks
- Bring your orthotics to every visit so we can check the fit
- Replace them when they wear out. See [how often to replace custom orthotics](/blog/how-often-should-i-replace-my-custom-orthotics).
