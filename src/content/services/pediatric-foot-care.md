## What it is

Skin and nail problems are some of the most common reasons people visit a podiatrist, and children have their own special foot needs as they grow. Most of these problems are easy to treat when they are seen early.

This group covers everyday family foot care, including corns, warts, athlete's foot, ingrown and fungal nails, and the flat feet, walking patterns, and growth-related pain that show up in childhood.

## What it feels like

- Hard, thick, or cracked skin, or a painful spot that feels like a pebble
- A red, sore toenail edge, or a thick, yellow, crumbly nail
- Itchy, peeling skin between the toes
- A lump on the top of the foot or ankle
- A child who trips often, walks with toes turned in or out, or complains of foot or heel pain

## Common causes

- Pressure and rubbing from shoes
- Viruses picked up in pools, gyms, and locker rooms
- Fungus that grows in warm, damp shoes
- Cutting nails too short or curved
- Injury to a nail
- Growth, inherited foot shape, and muscle tightness in children

## How we diagnose it

We look closely at the skin and nails, and if needed test a small sample to confirm a fungus or wart. For children we check the shape of the foot, how the legs and feet line up, and how they walk and run. Most exams are quick and gentle, and X-rays are taken only when needed.

## How we treat it

Treatment is often simple and done in the office. We trim corns and calluses, treat warts with medicine or freezing, and clear athlete's foot and nail fungus with the right topical or oral care. We numb the toe and remove the sore part of an ingrown nail for fast relief, and can treat the nail root so it does not come back. For children, we may suggest stretches, supportive shoes, inserts, or activity changes, and we keep watching them as they grow.

Most children outgrow common problems like mild flat feet or toeing in. When a problem does not improve, causes pain, or affects walking, we discuss more support or, rarely, surgery. Stubborn warts, cysts, or ganglions can also be removed in a short procedure.

## Recovery and what to expect

Skin and nail treatments bring relief quickly, often the same day. Nail fungus grows out slowly, so it can take several months to see a clear nail. For children, many concerns settle with time and support, and we schedule follow up visits to make sure feet are growing well.

## Tips to prevent it

- Cut nails straight across and not too short
- Keep feet clean and dry and change socks daily
- Wear sandals in public showers and pools
- Choose shoes that fit, and check children's shoes often
- Do not pick at warts or cut corns yourself

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if a spot on the foot hurts or changes, a nail is red, swollen, or draining, or your child has foot pain, trips often, or walks differently. See us sooner if you have diabetes.
