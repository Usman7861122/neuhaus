## What it is

The front of the foot has many small joints and bones that carry your weight when you push off. Shoes that squeeze, foot shape passed down in families, or extra pressure can make the big toe lean, bend the smaller toes, or leave the ball of the foot sore.

This group covers bunions, bent toes, stiff big toe joints, and the many causes of pain in the ball of the foot, including nerve, joint, and ligament problems.

## What it feels like

- A bump on the side of the big toe or the little toe
- Toes that bend, cross over, or curl under
- Burning, tingling, or a stone-in-the-shoe feeling in the ball of the foot
- Pain or stiffness when you push off or bend the big toe
- Corns or calluses where toes or the ball of the foot rub

## Common causes

- Family history and inherited foot shape
- Narrow, pointed, or high heeled shoes
- Flat feet or feet that roll inward
- Arthritis or injury to a toe joint
- Muscle imbalance or tight calf muscles
- Repeated pressure from running or standing

## How we diagnose it

We look at the shape of your foot, test how each toe joint moves, and check for sore spots and nerve symptoms. Weight bearing X-rays show the angle of the joints, and we may use ultrasound or MRI to check ligaments, the plantar plate, or a neuroma.

## How we treat it

Early problems often improve with wider shoes, soft pads, toe spacers or splints, custom orthotics, and metatarsal pads that take pressure off the ball of the foot. Ice, anti-inflammatory care, and targeted injections can settle a sore joint or nerve. We also trim painful corns safely.

When pain limits your day or the deformity keeps growing, surgery can realign a bunion, straighten a toe, repair a plantar plate, or release a nerve. Modern methods often mean smaller cuts and an earlier return to walking. We will explain your options, the recovery, and what to expect.

## Recovery and what to expect

Without surgery, most people feel better within weeks once they have the right shoes and support. After surgery, many patients walk in a special shoe soon after and return to regular shoes in about six to eight weeks. Full healing can take a few months.

## Tips to prevent it

- Choose shoes with a roomy toe box
- Avoid high heels and pointed shoes for long periods
- Use pads or spacers where toes rub
- Wear supportive inserts if you have flat feet
- Check your feet and shoes for changes every few months

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if a bump is growing, a toe is bending or stiff, or pain in the ball of the foot lasts more than a week or two. Early care gives you more options and often means simpler treatment.
