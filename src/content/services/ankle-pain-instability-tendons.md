## What it is

Your ankle is held together by ligaments and moved by strong tendons. A bad twist can stretch or tear a ligament, and repeated strain can irritate a tendon. If an ankle sprain never heals fully, the joint can feel weak and give way again and again.

This group covers sprains, chronic instability, tendon problems on the inside and outside of the ankle, and injuries to the joint surface and soft tissues.

## What it feels like

- Pain, swelling, or bruising around the ankle
- An ankle that gives way or feels wobbly
- Pain along the inside or outside of the ankle that grows with activity
- Clicking, catching, or a snapping feeling behind the ankle bone
- Stiffness or a deep ache that does not go away after an old injury

## Common causes

- Twisting or rolling the ankle
- Sports and uneven ground
- Old sprains that never healed well
- Overuse of a tendon
- Flat feet or high arches that change the load on the ankle
- Weak muscles and poor balance

## How we diagnose it

We ask how the injury happened and test stability, strength, and balance. We check the ligaments and tendons by hand and use digital X-rays in the office to rule out fractures. When needed we order ultrasound or MRI to look at ligaments, tendons, and the cartilage of the joint.

## How we treat it

Most sprains and tendon problems heal without surgery. Care may include rest, a brace or walking boot, ice and compression, taping, custom orthotics, and guided physical therapy to rebuild strength and balance. Injections can calm an irritated joint or tendon sheath.

If an ankle keeps giving way, a ligament is torn, a tendon is ruptured, or the joint surface is damaged, our foot and ankle surgeons can repair or rebuild the structure, often with small cuts. We also plan your return to sport, step by step.

## Recovery and what to expect

A mild sprain may take two to four weeks, and a severe one longer. Tendon problems often need six to twelve weeks of care. After surgery, expect time in a boot followed by therapy. We give you clear goals so you know when it is safe to run and jump again.

## Tips to prevent it

- Do balance and ankle strength exercises
- Wear supportive shoes for your sport
- Warm up before exercise
- Use a brace on uneven ground if you have had sprains
- Do not return to sport before the ankle is ready

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if you cannot walk normally, swelling stays after a few days, your ankle keeps giving way, or you have pain behind the ankle bone. Get care right away if you think a bone might be broken.
