## What it is

Your foot has more than 30 joints, and the ankle carries your full body weight. Arthritis wears down the smooth cartilage in these joints or inflames them, which causes pain, stiffness, and swelling.

This group covers wear-and-tear arthritis, arthritis after injury, inflammatory types such as rheumatoid and psoriatic arthritis, gout, and related joint problems such as bone spurs.

## What it feels like

- Aching or stiff joints, worse in the morning or after rest
- Swelling and warmth around a joint
- A grinding feeling or less motion
- Sudden severe pain, redness, and heat in the big toe (a possible gout attack)
- A bony bump or spur near a joint

## Common causes

- Wear and tear over the years
- Past injuries such as fractures and bad sprains
- Autoimmune conditions such as rheumatoid or psoriatic arthritis
- High uric acid, which causes gout
- Extra body weight
- Family history

## How we diagnose it

We check each joint for swelling, warmth, and motion, and ask about morning stiffness and past injuries. X-rays show narrowing of the joint and bone spurs. Blood tests, joint fluid tests, ultrasound, or MRI can help find the type of arthritis or confirm gout.

## How we treat it

Most patients start with non surgical care: supportive shoes with a rocker sole, custom orthotics or braces, activity changes, anti-inflammatory medicine, and gentle exercise. Cortisone injections can calm a flare. For gout, we treat the attack quickly and build a plan to lower uric acid and prevent the next one.

When pain remains despite good care, surgery may help. Options include cleaning out bone spurs, repairing damaged cartilage, joint fusion, or joint replacement in select cases. We will explain which choice fits your goals and your daily life.

## Recovery and what to expect

Arthritis does not go away, but it can be managed well. Many people stay active with the right shoes, support, and treatment. Gout flares often ease in a few days with treatment, and long term care lowers the chance of repeat attacks.

## Tips to prevent it

- Stay active with low impact exercise like swimming or cycling
- Wear shoes with cushion and a stiff sole
- Keep a healthy weight
- Limit alcohol and sugary drinks if you have gout
- Take prescribed medicine exactly as directed

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if joint pain lasts more than a few weeks, stiffness makes walking hard, or a joint is suddenly red, hot, and very painful.
