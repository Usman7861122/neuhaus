## What it is

The back of the foot takes a lot of load with every step. The heel bone, the arch, and the Achilles tendon all work together, and when one of them is strained, the pain can show up as a sore heel, a tight arch, or a stiff tendon behind the ankle.

This group covers the common causes of pain in the heel, arch, and back of the ankle, including plantar fasciitis, heel spurs, Achilles tendon problems, bursitis, and growth plate pain in children.

## What it feels like

- Sharp or stabbing pain under the heel, often worst with the first steps in the morning
- An aching or pulling feeling along the arch
- Pain, stiffness, or a thickened area on the Achilles tendon behind the ankle
- A bump or rubbing at the back of the heel
- A sudden pop or snap at the back of the ankle with trouble pushing off (a possible tendon tear)

## Common causes

- Overuse from running, walking, or standing for long hours
- Tight calf muscles or a tight Achilles tendon
- Flat feet or high arches that put extra strain on the tissue
- Worn out shoes or shoes with little support or a stiff back
- Sudden jumps in activity or a change in training
- Growth spurts in active children

## How we diagnose it

We ask when and where the pain happens, then press gently along the heel, arch, and tendon to find the sore spot. We watch how you stand and walk and check the strength and flexibility of your calf. When needed we take X-rays in the office and may order an ultrasound or MRI to look at tendons, the fascia, or a possible stress fracture.

## How we treat it

Most heel, arch, and Achilles problems get better without surgery. Care often includes calf and arch stretching, supportive shoes, heel lifts, custom orthotics, taping, and short courses of anti-inflammatory care. Night splints and walking boots give tissue time to calm down. For children with Sever's disease, we use heel cups, activity changes, and stretching so they can stay in the game.

If the pain has lasted for months, we can add cortisone injections, shockwave therapy, or regenerative options. A torn or ruptured Achilles tendon, or a heel problem that does not respond to good care, may need surgery. Our foot and ankle surgeons explain every option and what recovery looks like before you decide.

## Recovery and what to expect

Many patients feel clearly better in a few weeks and return to normal activity in two to three months. Tendon problems and stubborn heel pain can take longer. A tendon repair needs a longer, planned recovery with a boot and guided rehab. Sticking with your stretches even after the pain fades helps keep it from returning.

## Tips to prevent it

- Stretch your calves and arches every day
- Wear supportive shoes and replace worn out ones
- Warm up before exercise and build up slowly
- Avoid going barefoot on hard floors
- Keep a healthy weight to reduce strain on the heel

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if heel or arch pain lasts more than a week or two, keeps you from normal activity, or you feel a pop or sudden weakness at the back of the ankle. Do not wait if you have diabetes.
