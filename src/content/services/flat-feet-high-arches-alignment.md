## What it is

The shape of your arch decides how weight moves through your foot, ankle, knee, and hip. Very flat feet and very high arches both change how you walk, and over time the extra strain can cause pain far from the foot itself.

This group covers arch shape problems, alignment differences, and unusual walking patterns, in children, adults, and people whose feet have changed with age.

## What it feels like

- Feet that roll inward or outward when you walk or stand
- Aching arches, ankles, knees, hips, or lower back
- Shoes that wear down unevenly
- Tired feet by the end of the day
- A bony bump on the inside of the foot (accessory navicular)

## Common causes

- Inherited foot shape
- Weak or damaged tendons, especially the posterior tibial tendon
- Nerve or muscle conditions, especially with very high arches
- Injury or arthritis in the midfoot
- Differences in leg length
- Weight gain or long hours standing

## How we diagnose it

We watch you stand and walk, check the shape and flexibility of the arch, and measure how your feet and legs line up. Weight bearing X-rays show the bones under load. For advanced changes we may order an ultrasound or MRI to check tendons and joints.

## How we treat it

Most people improve with custom orthotics made from a scan or mold of the foot, supportive shoes, bracing, and exercises that strengthen the foot and ankle. For a painful accessory navicular, we can use padding, orthotics, and short-term immobilization. We tailor the plan to your foot, your job, and your activities.

When a deformity is advanced or painful despite good care, surgery can rebalance tendons, realign bones, or fuse a damaged joint. Our surgeons explain when it helps and when non surgical care is enough.

## Recovery and what to expect

Orthotics need a short break in period of one to two weeks, and many patients feel the difference within days. Bracing and exercise plans take a few weeks to show results. After surgery, recovery depends on the procedure, and we give you a clear timeline.

## Tips to prevent it

- Wear shoes with good arch support and a firm heel
- Use orthotics as prescribed and check them yearly
- Keep calf and foot muscles flexible and strong
- Get children's foot shape checked if you see rolling or pain
- Replace shoes that wear unevenly

## When to book a visit

See a podiatrist if your arches ache, your feet roll in or out, you have pain in the knees or back that may come from how you walk, or the inside of your ankle becomes swollen and sore.
