## What it is

Diabetes and poor circulation can damage nerves and blood vessels in the feet. You may not feel a small cut or blister, and it may heal slowly. Small problems can turn into serious wounds and infections, so regular expert care is one of the best ways to protect your health and independence.

This group covers routine diabetic foot care, nerve problems, wounds that will not heal, infections, circulation problems, swelling, and nerve entrapment.

## What it feels like

- Numbness, tingling, or burning in the feet
- Cuts, blisters, or sores that heal slowly
- Cold, pale, or bluish feet, or leg cramps when walking
- Swelling in the feet or legs
- Redness, warmth, drainage, or a bad smell from a wound

## Common causes

- Long term high blood sugar
- Narrowed arteries that reduce blood flow
- Loss of feeling, so injuries go unnoticed
- Poorly fitting shoes
- Smoking
- Vein problems, kidney disease, or lymph system problems

## How we diagnose it

We check the skin, nails, pulses, temperature, and the feeling in your feet using simple tests. We look at foot shape and pressure points, measure blood flow when needed, and examine any wound closely. X-rays and lab tests help find infection or bone involvement, and we work with vascular specialists for circulation testing.

## How we treat it

Care includes a full foot exam, safe nail and callus care, moisturizing advice, and help with shoes and therapeutic inserts. Wounds are cleaned and treated with special dressings, offloading boots, compression, and advanced wound care. Infections are treated quickly with the right medicine, and we coordinate with your primary doctor to control blood sugar.

For severe infection, poor blood flow, or deformity that keeps causing wounds, we work with vascular surgeons and our foot and ankle surgeons on procedures that restore blood flow, remove infected tissue, or reshape the foot. Our goal is to save the limb and keep you walking.

## Recovery and what to expect

With regular visits and good daily habits, most problems stay small. Wounds that get quick care often heal in weeks, though some take months. Keeping blood sugar and blood pressure in range makes every part of care work better.

## Tips to prevent it

- Check both feet every day, including the bottoms
- Wash and dry feet well, especially between toes
- Never walk barefoot, even indoors
- Wear clean socks and shoes that fit well
- Do not cut corns or calluses yourself
- Do not smoke, and keep blood sugar in your target range

## When to book a visit

If you have diabetes, get a foot exam at least once a year. Book a visit right away for any cut, blister, color change, swelling, or sore that does not heal, even if it does not hurt.
